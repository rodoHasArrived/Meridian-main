Review · templates/strategy-formation/ · StrategyFormation · StrategyOnboarding · StrategyBuilder · F-01 · F-02 · F-05 · F-06 implemented

The screen is well-modelled and badly budgeted.

The data model underneath Formation is the strongest in the suite: gates recomputed from live values, a material hash, hash-bound acknowledgment, freshness derived rather than stored. Almost none of that rigour reaches the eye. The interface spends its best real estate restating which object you are looking at and its worst on the decision you came to make.

Twelve findings below, none of which require a new component. They are budget, arbitration, and affordance problems: vertical space spent on identity, one fact counted three different ways in three different places, and gates that describe a blockage without offering a way through it. Graded on the system's ladder: Blocked = the user is misinformed or stuck; Action = real friction with a known fix; Review = a judgement call worth making deliberately.

Vertical budget§1 · F-01 – F-03

At 1920×1080 the first editable field sits below three stacked identity statements. On a 1440×900 laptop — the actual desk machine — the Mandate form opens off-screen, and the user's first act on a compose screen is to scroll.

F-01Three headers say the same thing before the form startsImplemented

What is there

WorkstationTopbar 48px WorkspaceContextBar 40px Eyebrow "Strategy Executor · Formation" h1 "Strategy formation" 52px ObjectHeader STR-0007 v3 86px Stage header + blurb ~70px = ~296px before field one Viewport left for the form at 900px: ~560px

The eyebrow, the h1 and the ObjectHeader are three renderings of one idea. ObjectHeader already carries type, id, name, version and condition — it is the component built for exactly this, and it is the one that gets pushed down.

What to do

Delete the eyebrow and the h1 from the scroll flow. The module is already named in the topbar (moduleLabel="Strategy Formation") and in the breadcrumb; naming it a third time in 22px display type buys nothing. Keep ObjectHeader as the page's sole title — set labelled-by to it for the a11y name.

Let collapse="auto" on WorkspaceContextBar actually collapse on scroll rather than reserving 40px permanently.

Recovers ~130px — enough to put Mandate's five fields and the consequence bar on one screen at 900px.

F-02Gate state is counted three ways, and no two agree on screenImplemented

What is there

status bar GATES 4/6 clear ObjectHeader Blocking gates 3 rail Gates · Mandate (this stage only)

All three are correct and mutually unreadable: four of six stages have no blocking issue, three issues are blocking, and the rail scopes to the current stage. A reader cannot get from "4/6" to "3" without knowing that one stage holds two issues.

What to do

Pick one unit — issues, not stages — and state it once. Status bar: 3 blocking · 1 warning. ObjectHeader's exceptions field should name the stages, not count anything: Blocking · Universe, Portfolio rules, clickable (the onOpenExceptions jump already exists and is good).

The rail keeps its per-stage scope but gains the global figure in its label so the two are visibly nested: Gates · Mandate (0 of 3 blocking).

F-03Proof freshness is stated four times in three vocabulariesAction

What is there

context bar DATA Stale ObjectHeader 0 of 4 run proofs current ContinuityFrame state=stale Builder badge freshBadge(...)

Four surfaces, one derived fact (freshnessFor). "Stale", "0 of 4 current", a frame state and a badge variant are four encodings of the same boolean-plus-count, and the user has to learn that they are one thing.

What to do

ContinuityFrame owns freshness — it is the component with the retry affordance and the coverage sentence. Everywhere else becomes a reference, not a restatement: the context bar's DATA cell and the ObjectHeader coverage field should both read from it and both scroll-link to it on click.

Say the consequence, not the state: "4 proofs stale — dataset changed" beats "Stale" in every one of the four places.

Arbitration§2 · F-04 – F-06

Where two things compete for one slot, the screen currently declines to choose: reversible and irreversible actions share a button, stages of wildly different heights share a container, and blocking issues share a presentation with satisfied ones.

F-04The stage body resizes under the user between stagesAction

What is there

.sf-panel{display:none} .sf-panel[data-on="true"]{display:grid} Mandate 5 controls ~330px Universe 9 controls ~470px Review grid+ack+log ~560px

Panels are display:none siblings, so the stage container takes the height of whichever panel is live. Advancing a stage moves the DecisionConsequenceBar — the primary button — to a new vertical position each time, and the rail's guard list jumps with it.

What to do

Give .sf-stagebody a fixed viewport (min-height at the tallest panel, or a scroll region) and pin DecisionConsequenceBar to its bottom edge. The primary action should occupy the same pixels at every step — it is the one control the user hits six times in a row.

Same for the rail: reserve the guard list's height so satisfied and blocked states do not shift the two panels beneath it.

F-05"Continue" and "Checkpoint → v4" are the same buttonImplemented

What is there

stages 1–5 primary = Continue · Thesis reversibility = null stage 6 primary = Checkpoint strategy → v4 reversibility = supersedable

One is free navigation between panels of a form. The other writes an immutable snapshot, bumps the version, and drives a lifecycle transition to validating → ready-for-review/blocked. They are rendered identically, in the same slot, in the same weight.

What to do

Navigation is not a decision. Demote Continue to a quiet stepper affordance beside the stage title (or rely on the rail, which already navigates), and reserve the DecisionConsequenceBar primary slot for the single irreversible act on the screen.

That also lets the bar state the consequence honestly at every stage: while composing, it can carry the blocking-issue count and what checkpointing would do instead of a next-panel label.

F-06Guards describe the blockage; one in nine offers a way throughImplemented

What is there

remediation: i.code === "worksheet-error" ? { label: "Open in Builder", actionId: "builder" } : undefined

Every other blocking issue renders as a sentence with a reason of Field · mandate.benchmark and no affordance — even though the issue object already carries the exact path of the field that would resolve it.

What to do

Every guard with a path gets a remediation: "Go to field →" that jumps to the owning stage and focuses that input. The data to do it is already in the issue; only the wiring is missing.

This is the single highest-value change on the list. It converts the guard rail from a report into the screen's primary navigation — which is what a gated compose flow wants it to be.

Affordance§3 · F-07 – F-10

Four places where the interface asks the user to know something it could show them: the shape of a filter, the cost of an edit, the meaning of a signature, and which of two worksheets is the real one.

F-07One worksheet, two incompatible renderingsAction

What is there

Formation · Portfolio rules stack of bare Inputs + ✓ / ▲ glyph Builder WorksheetGrid + cell inspector (statement · formula · functions · note)

The same portfolioRules.worksheet cells, addressed the same way (B5), presented as a form in one screen and a grid in the other. Formation's version cannot show cell dependencies, cannot show the inspector, and teaches a cell model the rest of the suite does not use.

What to do

Pick one. Preferred: embed the real WorksheetGrid read-only in the Portfolio-rules stage with the existing "Open in Strategy Builder →" as the edit path. The user then learns one worksheet idiom, and Formation's stage stays about rules in words — which is what its blurb promises.

If inline editing must stay, at least carry the ▲ cells' messages into the guard rail so a broken cell is a gate, not a footnote under an input.

F-08Universe filters are a regex the user has to guessAction

What is there

placeholder RATING >= BBB- parse /^([A-Z_]+)\s*(>=|<=|=|between)\s*(.+)$/i on failure "Format: FIELD >= value"

No field vocabulary, no operator list, no value validation, and no feedback on what a filter does. A user who does not already know the field names cannot author a filter, and one who does cannot tell whether RATING >= BBB- matched 900 names or nine.

What to do

Three controls instead of one string: field Select (from the chosen dataset's schema), operator SegmentedControl, value input typed to the field. The free-text box can stay as a power path, but it should not be the only path.

Then make each chip earn its place: RATING ≥ BBB- · −412 names. A filter list that does not show what it removes is a list of assertions.

F-09A material edit charges its cost silentlyBlocked

What is there

Changing universe.datasetId stales every run proof attached to the strategy. The warning is a sentence of body copy at the top of the stage — "editing them stales every run proof" — and the consequence lands silently in a rail panel and a context-bar cell the user is not looking at.

before 4 proofs · current after 0 of 4 current ← no acknowledgement, no undo, no diff

What to do

Mark material fields persistently — a Datum on the label, not prose in a paragraph — so the user knows which controls are expensive before touching them.

On change, surface the charge inline and reversibly: "Dataset changed — 4 run proofs stale · Revert". The store already has the previous value; the only missing piece is an undo affordance at the point of the edit rather than a state change three panels away.

F-10The most consequential act on the screen is the smallest controlReview

What is there

Acknowledgment binds a named actor to an exact formation hash — the record a reviewer later approves. It renders as a sentence and a 28px sm button inside a bordered strip, visually lighter than the Mandate name field.

.sf-ack 1px border, 12px text Button variant=primary size=sm

What to do

Give the review stage the weight of its content: show the hash being signed as a Datum, the actor, and — the missing piece — what changed since the last acknowledgment. "Stale, re-acknowledge" tells the user their signature lapsed but not what moved underneath it.

A two-column diff of the eight reviewItems against the acknowledged snapshot would make this gate self-evidencing instead of self-asserting.

Layout under pressure§4 · F-11 – F-12

F-11The rail gives equal weight to act-now, evidence, and metadataReview

What is there

ActionGuardList what blocks you ContinuityFrame the proof TimeContextPanel as-of / evidence-at / calculated-at sources

Three cards, identical treatment, stacked in authoring order. The third is provenance metadata that a user consults roughly once per session; it occupies the same visual budget as the list of things stopping them from finishing.

What to do

Rank them. Guards stay expanded and sticky. ContinuityFrame stays expanded while a proof is stale and collapses to one line when current. TimeContextPanel collapses to a single summary row — three timestamps agreeing is a one-line fact.

F-12Below 1180px the gates fall below the fold entirelyAction

What is there

@media (max-width:1180px){ .sf-grid{grid-template-columns:210px 1fr} .sf-rail{grid-column:1 / -1;flex-direction:row} }

The rail reflows to a full-width row after the stage body — so on the narrower of the two supported desk widths, the guard list sits below a 500px form. The thing that tells you why you cannot proceed is the last thing on the page.

What to do

At the breakpoint, promote guards above the stage body as a single-line summary bar (ActionGuardList show-summary already supports this), and let only ContinuityFrame and TimeContextPanel drop below. Stage navigation can become a horizontal stepper at the same width, which recovers the 210px column for the form.
